Solo vs. Group Adventures: Which One Is Right for You?

Lisa (Lead Explorer)
SoloLandscapes Field Team • 5 min read
Full Documentary Vlog • 14:25

Quick Field Takeaways (If you don't have time to read everything)

  • Solo Perk: Complete schedule autonomy & deeper local chats.
  • Group Perk: Shared transport costs & safer remote treks.
  • Location: Kampot River Trail & Chi Phat Waterfalls.
  • Best Month: July to November (Green & lush).

We built this company around the idea that traveling solo shouldn't mean traveling alone. That distinction is basically the whole answer to this question, but it's worth breaking down properly, because solo and group trips genuinely give you different things, and neither one is the better option across the board.

What Solo Travel Actually Gives You

Nobody to negotiate the schedule with. You linger at the waterfall as long as you want, skip the market stop if you're tired, decide on a whim to add an extra day in Kampot. Solo travel also has a way of pushing you into conversations you wouldn't otherwise have, with guides, with other travelers, with people in the village you're passing through.

There's a specific kind of confidence that comes from handling a new place on your own terms. The tradeoff is that certain trips are genuinely harder or riskier to do completely alone, remote treks, for example, where having other people around matters for both safety and morale on long, difficult days.

When a Small Group Trip Makes More Sense

If you are doing multi-day jungle expeditions in the Cardamom mountains where navigation is tough and emergency assistance is hours away, travel with vetted local guides and fellow explorers. You get the social camaraderie around evening campfires, split costs on private 4x4 transfers, and experience spots that solo logistics simply can't unlock.
